# Reading statistics and Home menu

The current interface has four tabs: Today, Stats, Calendar and Books, with day browsing, hours/minutes cards, reference illustrations, icon metrics, consistency, charts and completed-book history. See [the current refinement profile](study-suite.md#ui-and-game-refinement-profile-october-2026). Older three-tab descriptions below refer to the earlier arrangement.

Home starts with Browse Files, Recent Books, File Transfer, Reading Stats, Games, Apps and Settings, in that order. Open Settings > System > Home Menu to rename a row or show/hide rows. At most seven may be enabled. Settings is always enabled and may be renamed. The optional Plugins/OPDS row is available when plugins are installed or OPDS servers are configured. Clearing a custom name restores its translated default; names accept up to 48 UTF-8 bytes. Reorder Menu Items lets you select a visible entry and move it up or down. Settings can move but stays visible. Moves save immediately and apply to every Home layout. Hidden entries retain their relative slots until enabled.

Menu preferences live in `/.crosspoint/home-menu-a.bin` and `home-menu-b.bin`. Writes alternate between checksummed slots and are read back before the active settings change. A failed save leaves the previous configuration selected. Unsupported future formats are preserved and treated as read only.

Reading Stats has four tabs, in order:

- Today: browse individual recorded days and continue reading.
- Stats: lifetime reading time, sessions, average session, qualified forward pages, pages/minute, finished books and consistency.
- Calendar: the selected month, seven daily reading-time bars for the current week and six monthly reading-day bars. Tap a date for day details, a daily bar for day details, or the month heading/monthly bar for month details. Details keep all four tabs, previous/next period controls and Back to Calendar. Daily details include six four-hour time bins; weeks include daily minutes; months include reading days and consistency through the selected period's last date (or today for the current month). Future dates are unavailable. Week boundaries follow the Calendar app's week-start preference; week numbers use ISO 8601's Monday-based numbering, including for a Sunday-start display. The calendar browses the latest 730 days; detail totals are streamed from the full journal, including an entire boundary week/month. Filled days represent 30+ minutes, dithered days with positive reading under 30 minutes (including subminute sessions), and unfilled days no reading; a double border marks today.
- Books: Finished and Recent sections. Finished contains books that are currently finished, newest completion first. Reopening a book removes its finished entry; finishing it again restores one entry. Recent keeps a bounded window of up to ten unfinished documents, ordered by journal append order. Completing a document removes it from that window. Re-reading moves a document to the top and replaces its cumulative snapshot without adding a duplicate. Both categories show five books per page in portrait and three books per page in landscape, each in a single column. Compact total minutes appear above the min label; additional digits shrink to fit. Select a card for title/status, progress, total reading time, sessions, average session, pages/minute, estimated time left, first reading date and finish date (or last-read date for an unfinished book). The detail is refreshed from the latest valid cumulative snapshot. No reading records means no fabricated book statistics.

Left/Right switches tabs on button controls. Up/Down changes the calendar month or selected period, or selects a book; Confirm opens a selected book (or the displayed month's details). Back returns from a period to Calendar or a book detail to its same list/category/page, then to Home. Touch uses visible controls and the shared header Back target. Landscape arranges details in two columns and uses one upper Back control to retain readable metrics. Long book titles may be truncated to the available title area; control labels and metric names use the shared compact type scale.

## What is recorded

The reader's `ReaderSession` protocol counts every successfully rendered forward page, including rapid page turns in split-page manga. There is no minimum page dwell or session length. Time accumulates in milliseconds across pages and rounds up once per saved session to the journal's whole-second precision (at least one second for a session with pages). It never rounds each individual page to a second. Each page contributes at most 30 minutes. Backward turns, failed turns, refreshes and menu time add no reading pages. A session needs a trusted device timestamp. Time is active page dwell, rather than the entire period the book remained open. The final page without a subsequent forward turn does not add dwell time. Sync the device clock in Settings before reading.

In the reader, open the bottom panel > More > **Finish book** to mark an EPUB, XTC or XTCH as finished. Confirming saves 100% progress and adds the book to Finished in Statistics. This explicit action works even when automatic tracking for that format is disabled. It preserves real reading time, pages and sessions; it does not invent reading or reward PET with reading progress. The end screen has a Home button and a back arrow returning to the last page without changing the finished status. More then offers Mark as not finished. Confirming that action preserves the reading position and all recorded time, pages and sessions, and returns the book to Recent. Reading the last page keeps this choice; advancing to the end again or choosing Finish book marks it finished again. Cancel leaves the saved reading position unchanged. Set a valid date and time in Settings first.

Completion and reading position are separate microSD writes. If the completion is recorded but the position cannot be saved, the message explains that partial result; check the card and choose Retry saving finished position in More before leaving the reader. Completion and reopening records use the existing 384-byte journal entries and reserved metadata, preserving older reading sessions. Reopening cancels the finished count in the original completion period without removing reading activity. Older firmware ignores these manual records and can replace their book state if it records more reading. Back up `/.crosspoint/reading-history/` before downgrading, and avoid recording or syncing statistics with older versions. CrossPoint Sync includes manual completions in the existing statistics upload, and the saved 100% position participates in the all-books progress sync.

Settings > Reading > Statistics tracking controls which formats contribute statistics. XTC and XTCH share the XTC / XTCH switch and use the same session rules as EPUB, including touch swipes and physical page buttons. The switch does not control whether a book can open. Counters are saved when leaving the reader or preparing for sleep. A time display rounded to minutes can still show zero for a saved session under one minute, even though its pages and seconds are recorded. XTC/XTCH titles and authors use the metadata position declared by the file; older files without that offset retain the fixed metadata location.

A session is flushed on leaving the reader or preparing for sleep. The journal records its start timestamp, active duration, pages and final progress. Calendar/time-of-day distribution uses that start plus the active duration, splitting at midnight and four-hour boundaries. A completion belongs to its finish timestamp, including a completion exactly at midnight. Period sessions count sessions with positive reading time overlapping that period; a midnight completion can therefore appear on a day with zero sessions. Pages are apportioned by cumulative integer rounding, preserving their sum across adjacent periods. Pauses inside a session are compressed: the displayed day/time distribution is an estimate when a session spans long inactive gaps. Pages/minute counts rendered forward pages and changes with font/layout; it is not words/minute. A book is completed once recorded reader progress reaches 100%, unless it has explicitly been marked as not finished. Advancing to the end screen again clears that override. Re-reading the same document updates its statistics without adding a second finished-book entry. Existing reading before this feature was installed is available only if it was recorded locally or is present in the configured CrossPoint Sync account.

## CrossPoint Sync

Configure your account under Settings > System > CrossPoint Sync, using the CrossPoint server profile (or a compatible custom server). The circular-arrow shortcut appears immediately to the left of the battery in Reading Stats only while compatible credentials are configured. It is hidden for an unconfigured account or the strict KOSync profile. Open it and choose Sync; button users can hold Right in Reading Stats. A Wi-Fi picker opens if needed.

The same Sync button uploads this reader's saved cumulative statistics and daily reading totals, downloads the account's combined history, then synchronizes book positions across the microSD. The reader displays one unified statistics history. New local reading appears immediately without Wi-Fi; the next manual sync uploads it. Sync runs only when requested and does not modify the local session journal.

Book positions are synchronized for EPUB, XTC/XTCH, TXT and Markdown files in visible book folders, including books outside the ten-entry Recents list and files not yet added to the Library index. For each file, the furthest mapped position on the reader or server wins: a local advance is uploaded; a remote advance is saved for the next time the book opens. Books with no local or remote position are left unread. Filename and content identifiers are both checked, using an existing cloud record rather than creating a second one when it used the other matching method. The matching option in Settings chooses the initial identifier. Files absent from the card cannot have a position applied locally and their cloud records are retained.

Bulk sync always uses this furthest-position policy, including when the individual reader command is configured to ask. The book's own Sync Progress command remains available for explicit Apply remote / Upload local choices. Sync does not automatically download book files, or synchronize bookmarks and clippings as part of this bulk operation. Keep the same book edition on both devices for content anchors to match. If positions fail after statistics finish, the screen reports partial completion: completed work is kept, and retrying does not resend positions already equal. An intentional move backward can be handled through the individual command.

Every journal origin has a stable identifier derived from a device-specific secret and its first record. Repeating a sync replaces that origin's cumulative counters instead of adding them again. Only local counters are uploaded; downloaded totals are never uploaded as this reader's own reading. A downloaded snapshot contains the fingerprint and byte boundary of the local prefix already included. The offline view adds only later local records. Book snapshots and daily totals are combined too, rather than only the headline time.

A lost connection, cancellation, failed acknowledgement or incomplete response leaves the previous verified local snapshot selected. Some upload requests may already have reached the server; retrying them is safe with the same origin identifier. Before uploading, Sync checks the last verified local prefix and the origin's remote book/day counters. A rolled-back or divergent journal is rejected rather than lowering or orphaning the server's history. Incomplete final records are ignored without changing the journal; valid earlier reading remains usable. Starting a new journal creates another origin when its first record differs; previously synced reading remains in the account.

CrossPoint Sync can restore only statistics its clients actually sent. Reading-position history alone does not recover reading hours. Imported daily time is shown when available; missing hourly distributions, historical daily pages/sessions and unfinished progress are not invented. The unified Books list includes every imported/local book, split into Finished and Unfinished and ordered by document identifier. It is not a chronological Recent list. Calendar browsing retains the 730-day window; available older daily totals from 2020 onward remain accessible through the month/year picker. Imported 2000-2019 time contributes to overall totals but the current picker does not browse those dates. PET and Reading Quest rewards continue to use local reading events and are not granted retroactively from a cloud download.

The server must implement the [CrossPoint Sync statistics API](https://github.com/crosspoint-reader/crosspoint-sync/blob/main/docs/API.md#reading-stats), including cumulative global/book uploads and their acknowledgements. Strict KOSync servers synchronize positions but do not provide this statistics API. The transport reuses the existing account and firmware TLS implementation, including its current `setInsecure()` certificate-verification limitation; no credentials are stored in statistics snapshots.

### Saved data and limits

The local journal remains `/.crosspoint/reading-history/sessions.bin`. Downloaded data uses alternating, checksummed `sync-a.bin` / `sync-b.bin` snapshots in the same private folder; these include the account fingerprint and local checkpoint. Temporary `sync-response.json`, `sync-days.tmp`, `sync-finished.tmp`, `sync-unfinished.tmp` and `progress-response.json` files are owned by the manual sync flow. Book positions use the existing per-book `progress.bin` files and temporary-write/rename helper. As with individual reader saves, FAT replacement is not power-loss atomic; keep power connected when testing SD interruptions. Unsupported future snapshot versions are preserved. Removing the sync snapshots discards the offline cloud copy, which can be downloaded again; removing the entire folder also deletes local reading statistics. Back up that folder before modifying saves.

Limits are 20,000 distinct books, dates in 2000–2099, a 2 MiB response body, and a 2 MiB filtered JSON allocation budget. Uploads contain at most 20 dates or 10 books per request, with a checked request buffer capped at 16 KiB. Snapshots need 3,264 bytes plus 16 bytes per supplied date and 384 bytes per book, per slot. Import scratch can require one additional snapshot's payload plus the response file. A full sync rechecks/uploads cumulative counters; it does not keep a growing queue of duplicate events.

On X4 Pro, the combined projection uses a fixed hash index, an ordinal table and daily counters totaling under 2 MiB of PSRAM, with no internal-RAM fallback. Book bodies stay on SD. The combined-view workspace is released before pushing the sync flow so it is not retained alongside the uploader's projection. There is no extra task or framebuffer. The HTTP client is reused for one manual flow and released on every terminal result; radio ownership is released when leaving that flow. Runtime heap, e-ink timing and radio recovery still require device testing.

The statistics projection is released before processing positions. The position pass holds one book at a time, a 16 KiB request buffer, a 64 KiB JSON allocation budget, a 16 KiB response-file limit and up to 32 directory handles. Paths are limited to 1,023 bytes and the walk stops with an explicit error at 200,000 entries or excessive directory depth. It skips hidden folders and reserved app/resource folders at the root. It does not load the whole library into RAM or allocate another framebuffer. Book metadata is released before network requests; EPUB positions reuse the existing XPath mapper and fixed-page formats reuse their page counts. A large library takes longer because each book's two identifiers are checked over the network, but Back can cancel between books or during a request.

### Sync verification

On a test account, sync existing cloud history, read a few new pages offline, exit the reader, and check the increased unified totals. Sync again and expect the same totals; repeat without reading and verify no further increase. Repeat after restarting the device. Cancel a transfer, interrupt Wi-Fi after an upload, then retry; no local reading should disappear or be counted twice. Test both orientations, a multi-page Books list, midnight/day boundaries, changing accounts and an unconfigured account (no header shortcut).

Also test several books, including one absent from Recents and both XTC and XTCH. With an EPUB at 7% locally and 5% remotely, the same statistics Sync button should update the app to 7%. Advance another book remotely, sync once, then open it on the reader and expect the later position. Repeat without reading: neither positions nor statistics should increase. Interrupt Wi-Fi midway and expect a failure/partial-completion notice, then retry successfully. Check the saved position survives a reboot. These steps require an actual X4 Pro and a test account; automated loopback tests do not establish hardware behavior.

The host suites under `host-tests/reading-cloud` and `host-tests/reading-progress` test frozen local prefixes, repeated imports/uploads, acknowledged and lost uploads, rollback detection, damaged storage, allocation failure, date batching, book merging, bulk position discovery and retries, both languages and orientations. Their integration harness runs the production client, uploader, importer and position state machine against an isolated checkout of the official server. HTTP is real loopback traffic; host adapters replace device TLS, Wi-Fi, SD hardware and EPUB/XTC pagination with deterministic fixtures. Existing XPath/comparison suites cover the shared mapping helpers; the complete simulator build compiles the actual parser and mapper integration. Physical TLS, SD interruption and screen timing remain unverified by these tests.

## Persistence and resources

The authoritative append-only journal is `/.crosspoint/reading-history/sessions.bin`, with a 16-byte versioned header and 384-byte checksummed records. Every record also holds a cumulative book snapshot, so disposable per-document `.idx` files can be rebuilt. Missing/stale indexes fall back to a sequential scan. A partial final record is discarded on the next append; invalid checksummed records are skipped without a persistent list banner, while I/O errors and unsupported headers stop the scan. A power cut can lose the unflushed session. Previous valid records remain available.

The activity owns one fixed 730-day summary, two bounded period accumulators, ten reusable book snapshots plus one detail snapshot and reusable UI descriptors. These replace the previous second 730-day summary and six-entry page buffer. No journal format change or migration is needed. Month consistency joins the full journal's active-date intervals backward from the selected cutoff; an extra pass joins intervals that were appended out of date order after a clock change. These extra passes do not count aggregates again. It scans at most 32 records per loop so Back remains available while a large journal loads. Reader flush allocates one bounded repository workspace with `makeUniqueNoThrow`, checks allocation failure and releases it on return. No per-book array grows with the library. The journal grows by 384 bytes per session; it currently has no compaction. For ten sessions/day that is about 1.4 MB/year on SD. Clearing EPUB layout/font cache does not remove this folder; deleting the whole `/.crosspoint/` directory does.

The read-only reference firmware in `REFERENCIA FIRMWARE/XPoint-develop` inspired the statistics sections and presentation. The implementation is fork-owned under `src/apps_local/reading`; no build or runtime path requires the reference folder.

## Verification on hardware

Verify the seven default Home rows, with File Transfer third. Hide a row, enable a replacement, rename Settings, reboot and verify the same rows and dispatch targets. Attempt to enable an eighth or hide Settings. Check that a customized older menu retains its names, visibility and order. Restore the defaults afterward.

With a synced clock, read several forward pages, leave the reader and check that totals increase. Reopen and sleep to verify persistence without duplicate sessions. Finish a short book, open its entry in Books and inspect its individual statistics; revisit the end and verify the completed count stays unchanged. Compare reading around midnight with the session distribution definition above. Check calendar month boundaries/leap February, empty history, damaged SD handling and a missing index. Preserve a backup before intentionally damaging test data.

Repeat with both XTC and XTCH enabled: spend about 20 seconds on each of four pages, advance after each dwell, then leave the book. Expect at least one displayed minute, four pages and the book's title/author when present. Repeat using physical page buttons, and with the format switch disabled to confirm it adds no statistics. Repeat with short rapid swipes: every successfully rendered forward turn must now add a page, even when the whole session lasts under ten seconds. Reopening the book without reading must not duplicate its previous session.

Library metadata uses the separate Use Book Metadata setting. Refresh library after updating so existing XTC/XTCH files are indexed with their embedded titles and authors. A missing embedded author remains unknown; the firmware does not guess it from the filename or folder.

Exercise portrait, inverted portrait and both landscape orientations, long/accented titles, selectable dates/weekly/monthly bars, period Back/previous/next controls, first/last history month, both Books categories and their pagination and Back while the loading screen is visible. Repeat reader/statistics entry and exit while monitoring free heap, largest block and task stack high-water marks through debug logging. Browser/host tests do not establish physical e-ink timing or real SD power-loss behavior.

Home shortcuts use the same seven-item limit, including the required Settings row. Once seven entries are enabled, hide one before adding another. In Show / Hide Menu Items, + Add Game and + Add App open paged selectors of the installed registry. The main list shows only chosen shortcuts. Tap a shortcut there to remove it; Rename Menu Items also renames shortcuts. The shortcut stores folder plus stable item title, so adding/reordering games does not change its target. Opening a shortcut uses the shelf launcher; Back returns directly Home, including after sleep/reboot; launches from Games or Apps still return to their folder. Folder visibility and Home shortcuts are independent.

Menu preferences upgrade HMC1/HMC2/HMC3 to HMC4 on the next change. An untouched six-entry default menu gains File Transfer in third position; customized names, visibility and order remain intact. Hiding File Transfer explicitly in HMC4 stays respected on subsequent loads. Two 992-byte verified SD slots store at most five shortcut identities, with no per-frame allocation. Thirteen ordered item IDs fit the reserved bytes, so the slots remain 992 bytes. Earlier firmware treats HMC4 as unsupported and preserves it instead of overwriting the expanded configuration. Settings remains reachable through the default fallback.


In Stats/Data, tap Total reading time to choose a month or a whole year from 2020 through the current date. The upper Back to Stats/Data block returns to that menu. Previous/next year controls select the picker year; future months are disabled. Details keep Stats/Data selected and retain the existing metrics and period navigation; Back returns to the picker, then Stats/Data. Annual detail charts show books finished per month with integer book counts, attributed to the local completion month. The annual accumulator retains twelve 64-bit reading-time totals and a 366-bit active-date map, and adds twelve 32-bit finished-book counts. The two activity projections add 96 fixed bytes without allocation. The journal and its formats remain unchanged.

The selectable total-reading-time card shows an outlined calendar button in its upper-right corner. Tap the button or anywhere on the card to open the period selector. The button appears only when period selection is available.

The chapter sheet uses four rows per page in portrait and landscape, regular document-selector type for chapter names and the existing Roman-numeral type. Long names can wrap to two lines.

The Home-order buttons reuse an activity-owned SDK descriptor while the settings screen exists; it is released with that activity and adds no per-render allocation.

The Today goal card displays only its configured target, such as 30 min; the reading-time card above shows the recorded time for that day. Its progress bar still reflects actual reading time. Open the goal calendar to change an existing target.
