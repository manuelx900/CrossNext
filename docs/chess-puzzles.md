# Chess Puzzles

Every visit opens difficulty selection. Choosing Easy, Medium or Hard opens a freely selectable numbered grid: 16 problems per page, 400/350/250 problems respectively, with current/total page controls. An unfinished last problem in that group appears as Continue problem above the grid; it never bypasses difficulty selection. Completed problems carry a check and remain replayable. The stored number reopens its initial board after leaving the activity; piece moves are not persisted. Back from a board or completion opens the picker, then difficulty, then the normal Chess menu. The normal game remains resident and unchanged.

The same 1,000 CC0 [Lichess positions](https://database.lichess.org/#puzzles) remain: 400 Easy (rating <=1400), 350 Medium (1401–1999), 250 Hard (2000+). Medium/Hard exclude mate-in-one. Targets are mate in one, two or three player moves. The counter counts player moves only, including moves away from the source solution. Ratings describe source difficulty, not an exact effort estimate.

Tap any owned piece and a legal target. Legal moves are played; only illegal chess moves are rejected. The header shows player moves used against the target; play continues after exceeding that target. The opponent follows the supplied reply while on that line; after a deviation the existing ChessEngine chooses from the actual position, depth three with a 6,000-node budget. This bounded offline opponent does not prove all forced mates. A Thinking frame precedes search. Search lists are activity-owned; there is no worker, network request, per-move heap growth or extra framebuffer. Physical latency/stack measurements remain pending.

Promotion offers Queen, Rook, Bishop and Knight. Any player checkmate completes the puzzle, including alternate mates or mate over target; the counter lets the user judge the attempt. Opponent checkmate, stalemate and the fifty-move limit end the attempt. Threefold repetition is not adjudicated. Restart resets the board/counter. A win shows a knight completion badge, objective/move counters, group progress, Next problem, Review Board and Choose problem; repeat solutions never give duplicate credit. Completing all 1,000 displays a completion message and offers Reset All Progress.

The board’s Choose button and completion screen open the current group’s numbered picker. All tiles are available immediately. Prev/Next and side keys on the board wrap within the current category. In the numbered picker, side keys change pages without selecting a problem; holding a key changes one page without another step on release. Picker arrows and side keys stop at the first and last page. Difficulty and reset choices remain touch controls. Reset All Progress asks for confirmation and affects puzzle results/cursors only, never the normal chess game.

Two 152-byte CPZ2 slots remain at /.crosspoint/chess-puzzles-{a,b}.bin, with generation/checksum and verified readback. CPZ2 adds the last global number in reserved bytes. CPZ1 results/cursors migrate; because CPZ1 had no active category its Easy cursor is the initial resume position. Navigation debounces 1.5 seconds; exit, sleep and completion flush. No-op saves do not write. Torn latest saves recover the other valid slot; damaged/future records stay read-only until explicit reset. Reset removes only these two files. Catalogue order remains part of the contract: replacement requires ID migration or a version change.

Source IDs/FENs/ratings/lines stay pinned in tools_local/chess/puzzles.json. Import defaults to offline validation/regeneration; only --refresh replaces data. The source-line promotion exclusion remains, while free play supports promotions. The importer never runs in firmware builds. Tests replay all source mates, accept other legal moves, exercise replies/promotions/over-target play and storage/reset. The reference firmware folder is unused.

Normal Chess and puzzles use the same Classic reference pieces. Legacy Pixel/Ornate preferences fall back to Classic and there is no design picker. `tools_local/chess_reference/generate.py` regenerates the immutable 64px masks from `assets_local/chess_reference/`. Rendering adds no heap buffer. Completed picker cells have a black background and white check; unfinished cells remain outlined, with the current selection filled separately. Puzzle data and progress formats are unchanged.

On X4 Pro check both colours/four orientations, remapped buttons, off-line moves, replies, promotions, over-target move count, restart and win/loss/draw. Visit a problem in each group, exit/reopen/reboot: expect difficulty, then that group’s picker and Continue problem. Test first/last picker pages and all group boundaries, duplicate completion, Reset/Cancel and preservation of the normal game. Use disposable SD data for torn saves/reset errors. Measure internal heap, largest block and loop/render stack high-water during repeated entry/search. Host/browser checks establish no hardware measurements.

The difficulty menu shows a single total count, three group counts without progress bars, the reference knight-on-board illustration and Reset progress at the bottom. The puzzle dataset, group sizes and progress format are unchanged.
