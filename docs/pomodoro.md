# Pomodoro

Open **Apps > Pomodoro**. Defaults are 25 minutes of focus, a 5-minute short break and a 15-minute long break after every four planned focus stages. Start/Pause controls the current timer. Skip asks for confirmation, advances in pause and does not credit a completed focus. Restart asks for confirmation and restarts the current phase in pause. Both preserve studied time and the completed-session count.

The header has a calendar shortcut immediately left of the settings gear. Today’s date appears above the stage strip. The larger countdown has no enclosing card; Start/Pause and the outlined Skip/Restart buttons sit at the bottom. The compact accumulation card centers the selected period name and study time: today, this week, this month or this year. Its outlined arrow opens four choices inside the card; choosing one saves it as the default. The initial choice is this week, Monday through Sunday, with no extra date range or session count on the card. Settings > Show accumulated study hides or restores it and saves that choice. There is no progress bar or all-time total on the timer. Square/rounded appearance and the selected UI font follow the shared Focus controls.

Settings pauses the timer. Focus is adjustable from 1–180 minutes, short breaks from 1–60, long breaks from 1–120 and the long-break interval from 1–12 planned focus stages. Changing the current phase's duration restarts it in pause. Auto Start Breaks and Auto Start Focus are independent and off by default. Completion advances to the next phase and shows a notice; press Start unless its automatic-start setting is enabled.

Settings uses the same compact duration panel as Focus: four labeled rows with outlined −/+ controls, values and minute/session units. Automatic start has its own section with separate Start breaks and Start focus switches. A filled switch means On. Done saves and returns to the timer. The layout adapts to portrait/landscape, the current UI font and square/rounded appearance.

The timer runs while the app is open and prevents automatic sleep while running. Leaving or manually sleeping pauses and saves. It is not a background alarm while reading or sleeping, and does not play a sound. Time in a temporarily opened control-center panel is counted on return; at most one completed phase advances, avoiding credit for unseen focus sessions.

The automatic-sleep hold applies while Pomodoro is the foreground screen. If the control-center panel remains open, that panel's normal idle timeout applies; sleeping then pauses and saves the underlying timer through the activity stack's existing sleep hook.

Timing uses unsigned monotonic elapsed milliseconds rather than subtracting one second per repaint. The screen refreshes when the displayed second changes; one in sixty paints requests a cleanup. Physical refresh rate, power cost and readability remain unmeasured.

The calendar opens on **Day**. Its arrows reach yesterday and earlier days; **Month** shows a Monday-first calendar with a heatmap of completed sessions (white: 0, light gray: 1, dark gray: 2–3, black: 4 or more), with a legend below, and **Year** shows monthly study time. Tap a date or month to open its detail, including empty dates. Dates can be browsed between 2020 and 2199. Today returns to the current day. Day totals include only that date; its seven bars provide the surrounding week’s study time. Year bars are labeled 1–12, January to December. Statistics keep an already running timer active; returning to it does not pause it. The accumulation card, totals and study bars exclude the current running focus segment. They publish its elapsed time only when that segment finishes or is interrupted by pause, settings, stage selection, confirmed skip/restart, exit or sleep. Only the countdown updates in real time. Resuming after a pause starts a new unpublished segment, so time is never credited twice. Physical navigation buttons move the selected period while statistics is open.

Only time actually consumed by a focus phase counts as study, including partial focus time before pause, skip, restart or stage selection. Breaks do not count. A completed focus increments the calendar date on which it ends; skipped or reset focuses do not. Midnight splits focus time between the two dates. Calendar assignment follows the device clock’s precision (the RTC fallback is cached for up to ten seconds); elapsed study time itself uses monotonic milliseconds. Periodic checkpoints still preserve the running segment for recovery; restarting the app after an interruption publishes the last saved partial study time. A missing date or a clock discontinuity keeps time and completion credit undated instead of inventing a calendar date. The timer remains usable and the calendar explains that the device date needs setting. Legacy saves contain no dated study history: their all-time count is preserved internally, while dated totals begin with this version.

The activity owns fixed state, interactions, pending credit, one active-credit mask, two 31-bucket projections and SD workspace. No worker, network, second framebuffer or per-tick container allocation is added. The existing checked nothrow activity allocation owns the workspace only while Pomodoro is open; a static assertion bounds the entire activity below 12 KiB. Host ABI measurements: 24-byte timer state, 112-byte pending-credit object, 408-byte projections and a 4,336-byte repository including one reusable 3,840-byte read-ahead block. Internal heap, PSRAM, largest-block and stack high-water measurements still require a device.

The canonical save is `/.crosspoint/pomodoro-history.bin`: a 16-byte POMH v1 header followed by checked 160-byte PHJ1 checkpoints. Each checkpoint holds the existing 64-byte POM2 timer encoding and study deltas plus the accumulation display preferences together, so a reboot cannot replay one save without its matching credit. Loading searches the last sixteen complete checkpoints for a valid record and restores the timer in pause. Truncated tails are replaced on the next successful save. An incomplete initial header is recoverable only when every available byte matches the known header prefix; legacy state is imported again and the next save rewrites that incomplete header. Unknown versions, unreadable history and corruption encountered inside a history scan remain protected from writes and display an unavailable message. Writes, checked close and readback verification must all succeed before pending credit is cleared. Unchanged checkpoints do not write; running saves retain the 30-second throttle, 350-ms edit debounce and flush on pause, completion, confirmed skip/restart, exit and sleep. An abrupt power loss can lose up to the most recent unsaved interval.

Without a canonical history file, the two legacy `pomodoro-a.bin` / `pomodoro-b.bin` POM1/POM2 saves are imported through the existing repository. They remain untouched as legacy files. Once a valid journal exists it is authoritative; older firmware will not see newer progress. To reset intentionally, back up and remove all three files while powered off. Do not remove the rest of `.crosspoint/`.

History is streamed in bounded batches, with one reusable SD read-ahead block. Input and timer updates are serviced between batches; very long histories take longer to project and show Loading until complete. Each active timer hour adds about 19 KiB at the 30-second checkpoint interval, plus explicit edits and transitions. Records have no automatic retention cutoff. Four pending date buckets cover normal midnight crossings; if storage stays unavailable through more than four distinct dates, further unmatched dates retain their credit in the undated bucket rather than growing RAM. Calendar scans use a fixed journal endpoint plus live, unsaved deltas to avoid counting a save twice while the timer continues.

Host checks: `.venv/Scripts/python.exe host-tests/pomodoro/run.py` on the prepared Windows environment, or `python3 host-tests/pomodoro/run.py` with a native C++ compiler elsewhere. `--render <directory>` emits seeded PBM previews using the actual FreeInkUI builders and fonts. The suite covers migration, partial study, breaks, midnight, clock jumps, leap years, all short checkpoint writes, failed close/readback, retry/reboot credit and both orientations, languages and UI font styles.

Device checks: compare a one-minute focus/break cycle to a separate clock; test long-break intervals and both automatic switches. Pause during ordinary and cleanup paints. Exit/re-enter and sleep/wake should preserve the remaining time in pause. Missing SD should show Save Unavailable without crashing. Run a partial focus, pause, confirm Skip/Restart and verify that minutes remain while no extra completion is awarded. Finish one focus and compare Today, Yesterday, Month and Year totals after exit/re-entry and a reboot. Check a midnight crossing, both automatic-start switches, calendar taps and physical period navigation. Historical totals before this update must not appear on invented dates. Verify portrait/landscape controls, then monitor heap/largest block and render-stack high-water mark across repeated open/close cycles. Hardware verification is pending.

The separate stage strip above Focus shows the programmed cycle: numbered focus periods alternating with short breaks, ending in a long break. Three stages are visible at once; arrows expose the rest (up to 24 stages). The active stage is black. Tap a different stage to start its full programmed duration in pause without earning completed-focus credit. Tapping the active stage does nothing. Skip follows this same planned order; elapsed focus completion earns credit. Choosing a different stage changes where the next automatic transition goes. The stage and remaining time survive exit/restart. Changing the cycle length clamps the stage into the new cycle and resets it if its phase changes.

A short physical navigation-button press toggles Start/Pause on the timer
screen. A second press pauses the running phase; it does not select another
control. Stage-strip arrows share the strip's outer frame and use vertical
separators rather than individual small boxes. Timer settings remain touch controls; the header calendar/gear have independent touch targets. The stage strip uses partial separators and omits separators adjacent to the selected black stage.
